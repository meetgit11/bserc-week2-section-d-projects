## 1. The laws of thermodynamics

- **Zeroth law:** If two bodies are each in thermal equilibrium with a third body, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This is what makes temperature a meaningful, measurable quantity.
- **First law:** Energy is conserved. For a system, ΔU = Q − W, where ΔU is the change in internal energy, Q is the heat added to the system, and W is the work done *by* the system. Energy can change form, but it cannot be created or destroyed.
- **Second law:** The entropy of an isolated system never decreases. Heat flows spontaneously only from a hot body to a cold body, never the other way round on its own. This is why no engine can be 100% efficient.

## 2. Heat engines and the Carnot cycle

- A heat engine takes heat Qh from a hot reservoir at temperature Th, does work W, and rejects waste heat Qc to a cold reservoir at Tc.
- The **Carnot cycle** is an idealised, fully reversible cycle (two isothermal steps, two adiabatic steps). No engine working between the same two temperatures can be more efficient than a Carnot engine.
- **Carnot efficiency:** η = 1 − Tc / Th, with temperatures in kelvin. Efficiency rises if Th is raised or Tc is lowered.
- Real engines are always less efficient than Carnot because of **friction, heat loss, and irreversibilities** — but even a perfect, frictionless engine cannot beat Carnot efficiency, because the second law forbids it.

## 3. Entropy

- **Entropy (S)** is a measure of disorder, or of how spread out energy is. The second law says the total entropy of an isolated system can only stay the same or increase.
- In a reversible process, ΔS = Qrev / T. In an irreversible process, total entropy increases.
- Example: when ice melts in a warm room, the entropy of the ice increases more than the entropy of the room decreases, so total entropy rises.

## 4. Key terms to remember

- **Isothermal:** constant temperature. **Adiabatic:** no heat exchange with the surroundings.
- **Internal energy (U):** the total energy stored inside a system (molecular motion + interactions).
- **Kelvin:** absolute temperature scale; 0 K = −273.15 °C. Always use kelvin in the Carnot formula.
